## 1. Nigeria mandatory e-invoicing (NRS Merchant Buyer Solution, MBS)

### Timeline by taxpayer size
| Segment | Annual turnover | Go-live | Enforcement | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Large | above ₦5 billion | November 2025 | From April 2026 | https://sovos.com/regulatory-updates/vat/nigeria-nrs-publishes-e-invoicing-timeline/ [ADVISOR, 2026-02-26] |
| Large | ₦5 billion or more | (already live) | Full-compliance deadline 31 July 2026 | https://www.financialnigeria.com/nrs-sets-july-31-deadline-for-large-taxpayers-to-fully-adopt-e-invoicing-system-news-3042.html [PRESS, 2026-07-19] |
| Medium | ₦1 billion to ₦5 billion | 1 July 2026 | From January 2027 (Jan to Mar 2027) | Sovos, above; https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/26/nigeria-e-invoicing-medium-taxpayers-nrs-mbs-guide [ADVISOR] |
| Emerging / small | below ₦1 billion | 1 July 2027 | From January 2028 | Sovos and Beancount, above |

- Underlying notice: NRS public notice, 17 February 2026, signed by NRS chairman Zacch Adedeji. Source: Financial Nigeria (above); NRS notice https://www.nrs.gov.ng/news/news/119 [GOV, not opened]
- Medium taxpayers: post-go-live review "through October–November 2026", full enforcement Q1 2027. Source: Beancount (above). Status as of today (2026-11-12): INFERRED that medium taxpayers are inside the review window, about 7 weeks before enforcement starts.
- Large taxpayers: "over 1,000 companies had complied" as of Q1 2026. Source: Financial Nigeria (above).
- Large-taxpayer defaulters: "Any defaulting member may be subjected to appropriate regulatory and enforcement actions." (NRS statement via media adviser Dare Adekanmbi). Source: Financial Nigeria (above).

### What compliance requires
- Onboarding on MBS, integrating systems "through approved Access Point Providers or Systems Integrators", validation and testing, transmitting invoices, and receiving only invoices with valid reference numbers. Source: Financial Nigeria (above).
- A cleared invoice "comes back with an Invoice Reference Number (IRN) and a cryptographic stamp marking it authentic." Source: Beancount (above).
- Scope: "All VAT-registered suppliers are covered, including foreign entities supplying into Nigeria." B2B, B2G and B2C. Source: Beancount (above).
- Duplo's own FAQ: "NRS e-invoicing applies to VAT-registered businesses operating in Nigeria." and on PDF invoices: "No." Source: https://tryduplo.com/nrs-e-invoicing/ [SITE]

### Penalties
- Bypassing the system: ₦200,000 plus 100% of the tax due, plus interest at 2% above the CBN monetary policy rate. Source: Beancount (above) [ADVISOR].
- Blocking NRS fiscalisation deployment: ₦1 million first day plus ₦10,000 per later day. Source: Beancount (above).
- Techeconomy reports fines of ₦200,000 plus 100% tax surcharges on unreported transactions, for businesses with turnover above ₦1 billion. Source: https://techeconomy.ng/duplo-taps-dual-cbn-licenses-to-automate-tax-compliance-and-curb-500bn-revenue-loss [PRESS, 2026-03-20]
- Input VAT: an invoice without clearance "can cost you (and potentially your buyer) input-VAT credit." Source: Beancount (above). INFERRED: this pushes buyers to demand compliant invoices from their suppliers, spreading the mandate down the supply chain ahead of each segment's own deadline.
- Tax leakage estimate: "over ₦500 billion annually is lost to tax leakage" (NRS estimate as reported). Source: Techeconomy (above).

## 3. CBN cash policy (effective 1 January 2026)
- Weekly withdrawal limits: ₦500,000 individuals, ₦5,000,000 businesses; excess fee 3% (individuals) and 5% (businesses); ATM and POS count toward the cap; deposit caps scrapped. Sources: https://www.channelstv.com/2025/12/03/cbn-revises-cash-withdrawal-rules-effective-january-2026 [PRESS, SNIPPET]; https://cowrywise.com/blog/cbns-new-cash-withdrawal-limits-explained-what-nigerians-need-to-know-in-2026/ [SNIPPET]
- INFERRED: cash-heavy B2B sectors (FMCG distribution, Duplo's original market) face a cost on cash, which supports digital collections and bulk payouts.
